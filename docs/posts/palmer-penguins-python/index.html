Palmer Penguins: Does Bill Length Predict Body Mass?

Written in Python

Author

Jonah Sider-Echenberg

Published

September 24, 2026

Introduction

Penguins come in different shapes and sizes depending on species, and it’s natural to wonder whether a simple external measurement — bill length — says anything about a penguin’s overall body mass. In this post I explore that question using the Palmer Penguins dataset.

Data source: Palmer Penguins, collected by Dr. Kristen Gorman at the Palmer Station Long Term Ecological Research Network in Antarctica, and distributed as the palmerpenguins Python package.

import pandas as pd
import plotly.express as px
from palmerpenguins import load_penguins

penguins = load_penguins()
penguins = penguins.dropna(subset=["bill_length_mm", "body_mass_g", "species"])

The setup chunk above loads the data and drops rows with missing values in the columns we need. Warnings and messages are suppressed here.

Summary statistics by species

Before looking at any relationship, it’s worth checking how bill length and body mass differ across the three species in the dataset.

summary = (
    penguins
    .groupby("species")[["bill_length_mm", "body_mass_g"]]
    .agg(["mean", "std"])
    .round(1)
)

Gentoo penguins stand out immediately as the heaviest species by a wide margin, while Adelie and Chinstrap are closer to each other in body mass despite having noticeably different bill lengths.

Visualizing the relationship

penguins_length_to_mass = px.scatter(
    penguins,
    x="bill_length_mm",
    y="body_mass_g",
    color="species",
    trendline="ols",
    labels={
        "bill_length_mm": "Bill length (mm)",
        "body_mass_g": "Body mass (g)"
    }
)

penguins_length_to_mass.update_traces(
    marker=dict(size=8),
    selector=dict(mode="markers")
)

penguins_length_to_mass.update_layout(
    template="simple_white",
    font=dict(size=18),
    legend_title_text="Species"
)

penguins_length_to_mass

Figure 1 shows the scatter plot with a linear trend line fit per species. The positive slopes confirm that within every species, longer bills go along with higher body mass. Gentoo penguins occupy a visibly heavier and longer-billed region of the plot than Adelie or Chinstrap.

Conclusion

Bill length turns out to be a reasonable, if imperfect, predictor of body mass — both across species and within species. Species membership matters too: knowing only bill length without knowing species would give an incomplete picture than looking at the within-species trends shown here.
